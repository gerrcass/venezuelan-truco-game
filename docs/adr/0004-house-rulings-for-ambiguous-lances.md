# House rulings for ambiguous lances

Venezuelan truco varies by table, and the sources disagree on flor reservada, flor-envido betting, prive and several tie-breaks. Rather than hide those choices in code, the mesa adopts a written ruleset (`docs/rules/TRUCO-VENEZOLANO.md`): 24 piedras; flor reservada worth 5; both flors compared automatically (no flor-envido subgame), with the best flor of each team and a numeric tie to the mano; flor por derecho within the reparto; prive worth 1 at 23 and replacing envido; quemar supported; `a ley` and `barajo` flavor only; envido only on your own turn before the first card; the player who led a tied baza leads next. The prive and quemar are kept because they are distinctive Venezuelan texture, not because they are common.

**Timing rulings made explicit for ticket #5.** A flor is announced when declared but settles when the first baza closes (after the first trick is recorded, before the second), or at hand end if the reparto ends earlier; this gives both teams the whole first baza to declare, so play order never decides a flor comparison. A flor declared on your turn cancels an envido that was accepted or declined before it settles; a pending envido is answered first. Prive is called by the team at 24 − 1, not by either team: the other team's tanto answers. A flor, envido or prive that reaches 24 ends the partida immediately and the later truco payout is not made.

**Consequences:** the rules modal must cite the sources and the house rulings, so players are never surprised by a table convention. The hand summary must itemize every point source, because the settlement order is now observable. Any future ruleset variant needs a new ADR rather than a silent code change.
